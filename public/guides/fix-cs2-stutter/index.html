How to fix CS2 stutter and FPS drops

Hitches in smokes, frame drops in fights, a game that felt smooth before the last update? Here's how to find the cause and fix it, from a two-minute FPS cap to a clean driver install.

Updated November 8, 20267 min read

On this page

  1. Is it your PC or your network?
  2. Stutter right after an update
  3. Cap your FPS and fix V-Sync
  4. Lower the settings that cost the most
  5. Windows and driver fixes

Quick answer

  1. Turn on the FPS and packet loss telemetry first, so you know whether it's your PC or your connection.
  2. Set CS2 to your monitor's real refresh rate, turn off Wait for Vertical Sync and turn on NVIDIA Reflex.
  3. Cap your FPS with fps_max at a number your PC can hold most of the time.
  4. Update your GPU driver, then give CS2 a match or two to rebuild its shaders before you judge.
  5. Lower the expensive settings: shadows, particles, ambient occlusion and anti-aliasing.
  6. Close overlays and heavy background apps, and set Windows to its best performance power mode.
  7. Still stuttering? Verify the game files, remove your launch options and test again.

Is it your PC or your network?

"Stutter" covers three different problems, and each one has a different fix. Before you change anything, turn on the telemetry overlay in Settings → Game → Telemetry and show the frame rate, ping and packet loss. Then play a normal match and watch what happens when the game hitches.

  • Low FPS: the counter sits low all the time. Your hardware can't keep up with your settings, so lower them (see below).
  • Frame time spikes: the FPS looks high, but the game hitches for a split second, often when a smoke, molotov or flash goes off, or when you turn quickly. This is the classic CS2 stutter, and most of this guide is about it.
  • Network lag: the FPS stays steady, but players teleport, shots don't register or you get pulled back. Ping jumps or packet loss show up in the telemetry. That's your connection, not your PC. Skip to the network section.

Stutter right after an update

CS2 compiles shaders for your graphics card. After a game patch or a new GPU driver, that work starts over, and the first match or two can stutter badly while the shader cache rebuilds, especially the first time each effect appears on screen. It usually settles on its own, so don't start changing settings straight after an update. Play a couple of matches first.

If you have an NVIDIA card, check that the shader cache isn't limited: open the NVIDIA Control Panel → Manage 3D settings → Shader Cache Size and leave it at Driver Default or set it higher. Don't disable it. A cache that is too small forces the driver to rebuild shaders over and over, which feels exactly like random stutter.

Cap your FPS and fix V-Sync

An uncapped frame rate is not always the smoothest. On many systems the FPS swings between very high and much lower values, and those swings are what you feel as stutter. A cap that your PC can hold most of the time gives even frame times, and even frame times feel better than a higher average.

  1. Enable the console in Settings → Game → Enable Developer Console (~).
  2. Play for a few minutes with the FPS counter on and note the frame rate you hold in busy fights, not the peak in an empty spawn.
  3. Open the console with ~ and type fps_max followed by a value a little below that number. fps_max 0 removes the cap again.
  4. Set a lower cap for menus with fps_max_ui, so your GPU doesn't run flat out on the main menu.

Then go to Settings → Video and check the basics. The refresh rate must match your monitor; it is easy to end up at 60 Hz without noticing. Wait for Vertical Sync should be off, because V-Sync adds input lag. If you have an NVIDIA GPU, set NVIDIA Reflex Low Latency to Enabled. To keep the cap after a restart, put it in your autoexec or add +fps_max with your value to your launch options.

Lower the settings that cost the most

A few options in Settings → Video → Advanced Video are responsible for most of the frame time spikes in heavy fights. Smokes, fire and grenade effects are the worst moments, so start with the settings that touch them.

SettingTryWhy
Particle DetailLowSmokes, molotovs and explosions are the usual stutter moments.
Ambient OcclusionDisabledCosts performance without helping you see players.
Global Shadow QualityMediumOne of the heaviest options. Don't go lower than you need, because shadows can give away players.
Multisampling Anti-Aliasing2x MSAA or CMAA28x MSAA is expensive on mid-range and older GPUs.
High Dynamic RangePerformanceLighter on the GPU than Quality.
Shader DetailLowA cheap way to gain some headroom.
Dynamic ShadowsSun OnlyOnly if you still need FPS. "All" shows more shadows, which helps you spot players.

Leave Boost Player Contrast enabled unless you're desperate for frames; it costs little and makes players easier to see. FidelityFX Super Resolution is the last resort, because it renders at a lower resolution and the image gets softer. For a full walkthrough of every option, see our best CS2 settings guide.

Windows and driver fixes

Clean GPU driver install

Install the latest driver for your graphics card. If the stutter started after a driver update, or you've upgraded cards over the years, choose the clean install option in the installer so old profiles and settings don't carry over. Remember that the first match afterwards rebuilds the shader cache.

Power settings

Set Windows to its best performance power mode. Laptops should be plugged in, and in Settings → System → Display → Graphics you can tell Windows to run CS2 on the dedicated GPU by choosing High performance for cs2.exe. On battery or the integrated GPU, stutter is almost guaranteed.

Game Mode and GPU scheduling

Keep Game Mode on in Settings → Gaming → Game Mode. Hardware-accelerated GPU scheduling helps on some systems and hurts on others, so test it both ways for a few matches each and keep whichever feels smoother. It needs a restart to take effect.

Overlays and background apps

Everything that draws over the game or runs next to it takes a share of CPU time, and CS2 is sensitive to that. Try turning off the Steam overlay (Steam Library → right-click Counter-Strike 2 → Properties → General), the Discord overlay and any recording or performance overlay. Close browsers playing video, RGB and hardware monitoring tools you don't need and big downloads. If you run extra CS2 software, choose one that's built to stay light; that's how we build GCS Ware.

Check the hardware

  • Temperatures: a CPU or GPU that overheats slows itself down mid-match, which feels like stutter that gets worse the longer you play. Clean the dust out, check the fans and keep laptop vents clear.
  • RAM: make sure XMP (Intel) or EXPO (AMD) is enabled in the BIOS, otherwise your memory runs well below its rated speed. Two sticks in the right slots (dual channel) matter for CS2 too.
  • Storage: install CS2 on an SSD if you can, and leave some free space on the drive. Loading textures and shaders from a slow, full hard drive causes hitches.

Repair the install and reset tweaks

Corrupted or half-updated files cause stutter that no setting fixes. In Steam, right-click Counter-Strike 2 and choose Properties → Installed Files → Verify integrity of game files. Steam replaces anything that doesn't match.

Then strip back your own tweaks to rule them out. Clear your launch options and test with none at all; many "FPS boost" lists are leftovers from CS:GO that do nothing or make things worse (our CS2 launch options guide explains which ones still matter). If you use an autoexec full of old commands, rename it for a test session. When the game runs smoothly on a clean setup, add your changes back one at a time.

When it's actually your network

If the telemetry shows ping spikes or packet loss when the game feels choppy, no graphics setting will help. Try these instead:

  • Use a wired Ethernet connection instead of Wi-Fi if you can. Wi-Fi interference is a common cause of packet loss.
  • Pause downloads, streams and cloud backups on your PC and on other devices in the house while you play.
  • Restart your router and modem if the problem started suddenly.
  • If you have real, regular packet loss, set Buffering to smooth over packet loss in Settings → Game to one packet. It adds a small delay, so leave it off when your connection is clean.

Tip: change one thing at a time and play at least one full match before you decide whether it helped. Stutter comes and goes with the map, the server and the number of effects on screen, so a single round in an empty server tells you very little.
