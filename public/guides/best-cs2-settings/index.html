Settings

Best CS2 settings: video, display, audio and mouse for FPS and visibility

A menu-by-menu walkthrough for players who want more FPS, easier-to-spot enemies, or a bit of both. Every recommendation comes with the reason behind it, so you can adapt it to your PC.

Updated November 8, 20269 min read

On this page

  2. Best CS2 video settings
  3. FPS first or visibility first
  4. Game settings: telemetry and network
  5. Audio settings
  6. Mouse and sensitivity

Quick answer

  1. Set Display Mode to Fullscreen and Refresh Rate to your monitor's maximum, not 60 Hz.
  2. Use native 16:9 for the clearest image, or 4:3 stretched (for example 1280×960) if you want bigger-looking player models.
  3. Turn Boost Player Contrast on, Wait for Vertical Sync off and NVIDIA Reflex Low Latency on if you have an NVIDIA card.
  4. Keep shadows at Medium or High, because shadows can reveal players. Set shaders and particles to Low and turn ambient occlusion off.
  5. Use 4x MSAA as a balance, or None, CMAA2 or 2x on a weak GPU. Leave FidelityFX Super Resolution off unless your GPU really struggles.
  6. In Settings → Game, show FPS, ping and packet loss with Telemetry, and leave packet loss buffering off unless you actually have packet loss.
  7. Pick one sensitivity and stick with it. CS2 always uses raw input, so there's no mouse acceleration to fix.

Display, resolution and refresh rate

Start on the main Settings → Video → Video tab. These options decide how the image reaches your monitor, so get them right before you touch any graphics quality settings.

Display Mode

Choose Fullscreen. It has the lowest latency of the three modes. Windowed and Fullscreen Windowed are handy if you switch to other windows a lot, but you give up some latency for that convenience.

Resolution and aspect ratio

There's no single best CS2 resolution. It comes down to what you find easier to aim with:

  • Native 16:9 (your monitor's own resolution) gives you the sharpest, clearest image and the widest view to the sides.
  • 4:3 stretched, for example 1280×960, is popular because player models look bigger. In return, the image is softer and you see less at the edges of the screen.

A lower resolution also means fewer pixels for your GPU to draw, so it's a real FPS lever if your graphics card is the bottleneck. If you pick 4:3 and get black bars instead of a stretched image, check the scaling option in your GPU driver's control panel: it needs to scale to the full screen instead of keeping the aspect ratio.

Refresh rate

Set Refresh Rate to the highest value your monitor supports. Leaving it at 60 Hz by accident is a very common mistake on new setups. On a 144 Hz or 240 Hz screen, that means you only see 60 updates per second, no matter how high the FPS counter goes.

If CS2 launches into a broken resolution or the wrong refresh rate and you can't fix it from the menu, you can force them with launch options such as -fullscreen, -w 1920 -h 1080 and -refresh 240. The CS2 launch options guide explains each one and which popular options to skip.

Best CS2 video settings

Next, open Settings → Video → Advanced Video. This is where the graphics quality options live, and where you balance FPS against how easy enemies are to spot. The table below is a solid competitive baseline for most PCs.

FPS first or visibility first

Every competitive setup trades between two goals. If your PC struggles to hold a stable frame rate, go FPS first: smoothness and low latency matter more than anything you might spot in a shadow. If your PC has headroom, go visibility first and spend it on the settings that make players easier to see.

Shadows are a visibility setting

It's tempting to set every shadow option to the minimum, but a shadow can show you a player before you see the player. With Dynamic Shadows on All, the game draws shadows from all light sources, not just the sun, so you can pick up players you can't see directly yet. Sun Only is the FPS choice. Keep Global Shadow Quality at Medium or High either way.

Anti-aliasing and upscaling

Anti-aliasing smooths the jagged edges on models and map geometry. 4x MSAA is the common middle ground, and 8x is there if you have plenty of GPU headroom. On a weak GPU, step down to 2x, CMAA2 or None before you reach for FidelityFX Super Resolution. FSR renders the game at a lower resolution and upscales it, which costs sharpness, so treat it as a last resort.

Cap your frame rate

The menu settings decide how much work each frame takes. The frame rate cap decides how many frames you ask for. In the console, fps_max 0 removes the cap and any other number sets one. fps_max_ui does the same for menus.

Uncapped sounds best, but on some systems a very high or uncapped frame rate makes frame times uneven, and the game feels less smooth than the counter suggests. If that's you, set a cap your PC can actually hold in busy fights. A steady number often feels smoother than a higher one that jumps around. If you still get hitches with a stable cap, work through the CS2 stutter fix guide.

When the menu isn't enough

If you've lowered everything and FPS is still poor, the limit may be outside the video menu: Windows power settings, outdated GPU drivers or heavy background apps. You can also test the -vulkan launch option, which switches CS2 from the default DirectX 11 renderer to Vulkan. Results vary by system, so try both and keep the one that runs better for you.

Game settings: telemetry and network

A few options in Settings → Game are worth changing even though they have nothing to do with graphics.

  • Enable Developer Console (~): turn it on. You open the console with ~ and need it for commands like fps_max and sensitivity.
  • Telemetry: show the frame rate, ping and packet loss. You can display them all the time or only when the values are poor. It's the easiest way to tell whether a problem is your PC (low FPS) or your connection (high ping, packet loss).
  • Buffering to smooth over packet loss: leave it off (none) unless you actually have packet loss. Buffering adds a small delay to smooth things out, so it only pays off when your connection is dropping packets.

Skip the old CS:GO network commands

CS2 uses subtick, and official servers run at 64 tick. The CS:GO-era commands you still see in old configs, such as cl_updaterate, cl_cmdrate, cl_interp and cl_interp_ratio, do nothing useful in CS2, and neither does the -tickrate launch option. rate already defaults to its maximum (786432), so there's nothing to tune there.

Audio settings

Sound tells you where enemies are, so give Settings → Audio a few minutes.

  • Use stereo headphones. They're the recommended way to hear positional audio in CS2.
  • L/R Isolation and Perspective Correction both affect how the game places sounds around you. There's no universally correct value, so try a few combinations and keep the one that makes direction easiest to judge.
  • EQ Profile offers Natural, Crisp and Smooth. Pick the one where footsteps and gunshots sound clearest to you.

Test in a match or deathmatch, changing one option at a time.

Mouse and sensitivity

Mouse setup in CS2 is simpler than it used to be. The game always uses raw input and has no mouse acceleration options, so there's nothing to switch off. The Windows Enhance pointer precision option doesn't affect your aim in CS2 either.

That leaves two values that matter:

  • sensitivity: your base mouse sensitivity.
  • zoom_sensitivity_ratio: how sensitive the mouse is while scoped, relative to your base sensitivity.

You can set both in the settings menu, or type the command followed by your value in the console.

There's no best sensitivity. What matters is consistency: pick a value that lets you make both small corrections and quick turns, then stop changing it so your muscle memory can settle. If you ever change your mouse DPI, scale sensitivity the opposite way to keep the same feel (double the DPI, halve the sensitivity).

Save and test your settings

CS2 saves your settings to Steam Cloud and its own config files, but an autoexec gives you a portable, repeatable setup for a new PC, a reinstall or a settings reset. Put your console values (sensitivity, zoom_sensitivity_ratio, fps_max, fps_max_ui) in autoexec.cfg and load it with the +exec autoexec launch option. The CS2 autoexec guide walks you through creating the file.

To test changes properly:

  1. Turn on the frame rate readout in Settings → Game → Telemetry.
  2. Play the same map in the same mode before and after each change, ideally in busy fights, not while staring at a wall.
  3. Change one or two settings at a time, so you know what made the difference.
  4. Judge smoothness, not just the peak number. A steady frame rate usually feels better than a higher one that dips.

After a GPU driver or game update, the first match can stutter while shaders rebuild. That settles after a match or two, so don't change your settings because of it. If the stutter doesn't go away, the stutter guide covers the Windows, driver and hardware fixes.
